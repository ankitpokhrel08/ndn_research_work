# NDNsim v4 — figure descriptions (detection-floor study)

Caption-ready record of every figure in this folder. All are generated by
`notebooks/02_detection_floor.ipynb` from the 297-run rate sweep (`processed/full_sweep.csv`),
one unsupervised Isolation Forest trained on normal traffic only (t ≤ 200, pooled across
topologies), alarm at anomaly score ≥ 70. Saved at 200 dpi. Detector config:
`StandardScaler → IsolationForest(n_estimators=200, contamination=0.01, random_state=42)`.

Headline numbers used across the captions: **CP detection floor ≈ 44 Hz** (90% crossing),
**IFA floor ≈ 1.8 Hz**, held-out / benign FPR ≈ **1–2%**.

## Detection figures (`detection/`)

### `fig_floor_headline.png` — **the main result**
Attacker detection rate (%) vs attacker rate on a **log-x** axis, CP and IFA on one panel,
shaded ±1 SD band over topologies/seeds, the 90% line, and each attack's interpolated 90%
crossing annotated. **Tells:** both attacks have a real detection floor, and the two sit an
**order of magnitude apart** — CP ≈ 44 Hz, IFA ≈ 1.8 Hz. CP degrades gracefully; IFA holds
100% down to ~4 Hz and only breaks at ~1 Hz. *Suggested caption:* "Detection floor of the
unsupervised Isolation Forest against live background traffic. Cache Pollution, a purely
volumetric attack, has a high floor (~44 Hz); Interest Flooding, whose timeout/NACK signature
is qualitative, has a floor an order of magnitude lower (~1.8 Hz)."

### `fig_mechanism_contrast.png` — **why the floors differ**
Detection rate (%) vs **attacker interest volume as a multiple of the legitimate per-consumer
baseline** (log-x). **Tells:** IFA is already detected at ~1× the baseline (it barely has to
exceed the crowd), whereas CP needs ~10× before it is reliably caught. This is the
mechanistic explanation: CP's only signal is volume, so it must out-shout real traffic; IFA
leaves a qualitative fingerprint that survives at near-baseline volume. *Caption:* "Detection
vs attacker volume relative to the legitimate baseline. The qualitative attack (IFA) is
detectable barely above the crowd; the volumetric attack (CP) is not detectable until it is
~10× the baseline."

### `fig_detection_floor.png` — floor per topology, with seed spread
Two panels (CP, IFA). Per-topology mean detection curves (tree/dfn/dumbbell) with individual
seeds as translucent dots, the 90% line, the sub-90% region shaded, and the pooled crossing
marked. **Tells:** the curves for all three topologies are essentially superimposed and the
per-seed spread is tight — the floor is reproducible and topology-independent.

### `fig_floor_heatmap.png` — topology invariance
Two heatmaps (CP, IFA) of detection % over (attacker rate × topology), red→green. **Tells:**
the three topology rows are near-identical at every rate, so the floor is a property of the
**attack type and detector**, not the network topology. CP shows a smooth left-to-right
green-up gradient; IFA is green everywhere except the r1 column.

### `fig_score_separation.png` — score separation
Four anomaly-score histograms (attacker vs benign): CP @ 10 Hz (missed), CP @ 50 Hz
(detected), IFA @ 1 Hz (floor), IFA @ 8 Hz (detected), with the alarm line. **Tells:** at low
CP rate the attacker distribution collapses into the benign one (no separation → 21%
detection); at 50 Hz it sits cleanly above the alarm. IFA keeps separation even at 1 Hz,
where the attacker mass straddles the alarm (~60%).

### `fig_score_vs_rate.png` — score vs rate vs alarm
Mean attacker anomaly score vs rate (log-x) for CP and IFA, with the alarm line and the benign
95th-percentile band. **Tells:** the CP score slides down through the alarm as the rate drops
(crossing ~30–35 Hz), while IFA stays pinned near 100 until ~2 Hz and only approaches the alarm
at 1 Hz — the score-space view of the same two floors.

### `fig_score_timeline.png` — example onset detection
Attacker anomaly score over the full 600 s (8 s smoothing), tree topology seed 1, attack onset
at t = 300. CP panel: 50 Hz (detected, stays above alarm) vs 10 Hz (missed, stays below); IFA
panel: 8 Hz (detected) vs 1 Hz (floor, hovers at the alarm). **Tells:** at a high rate the score
jumps over the alarm the moment the attack starts; at the floor rate it never reliably crosses.

### `fig_feature_signature.png` — what each attack moves
Heatmap of the attacker's mean feature shift in the attack window, in std-devs from normal,
for the 9 detector features. **Tells:** CP drives the volume/data counters hard
(InInterests/OutInterests/InData ≈ +6σ) — a pure-volume signature; IFA's defining shift is
**timeout_ratio (+3.7σ)** with depressed satisfaction/data ratios — a qualitative,
black-hole signature. Explains why CP is volume-limited and IFA is not.

### `fig_fpr_vs_rate.png` — false-positive rate
Benign-consumer FPR (%) vs attacker rate, per topology. **Tells:** FPR is a real, low, and
**rate-independent ~1–2%** (tree/dfn ~0.8–1.0%, dumbbell ~2%) — not the trivial 0% the earlier
dead-traffic data produced. Confirms the floor numbers are measured at a sane operating point.

## Diagnostic figure (`diagnostic/`)

### `fig_legit_traffic_health.png` — the data is honest
Legitimate consumer interest rate over time for the three topologies, attack window shaded.
**Tells:** after the catalog fix (`NumberOfContents = 300`) the background traffic stays alive
and flat for the full 600 s — including through the attack window (~4.4 interests/s per
consumer) — so every detection number above is measured against a *busy* network, not the
silent one that confounded v1/v2. This is the validity check that makes the floor citable.
